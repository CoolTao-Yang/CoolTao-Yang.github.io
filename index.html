Research

A guiding question: How can we tell whether an agent has improved, and whether the evaluation itself deserves our confidence?

My work connects evaluation design, failure attribution, and data provenance. I use controlled comparisons and reproducible workflows to separate model limitations from benchmark defects. My mathematics background also informs an interest in structured physical priors, efficient attention, and environment and reward design for reinforcement learning.

Specific topics:

  1. Benchmark reliability and controlled evaluation
    Designing matched comparisons and auditing scoring rubrics to distinguish agent failures from conversion defects, answer leakage, and inconsistent evaluation criteria.
  2. Agent failure attribution
    Representing execution traces as decision graphs, developing failure taxonomies, and combining statistical attribution, graph-pattern mining, and targeted perturbations to investigate recurring failures.
  3. Reproducible synthetic tasks
    Building document-based workspaces and tasks with source-evidence mappings, schema-backed generation, temporal-consistency checks, and reproducible outputs. Making data provenance and quality controls explicit throughout the evaluation workflow.
  4. Structured priors for attention
    Incorporating physical priors into linear attention and reward shaping for reinforcement learning. In PhysEDA, separable Manhattan-distance decay reduces attention complexity from O(L²) to O(L). Representative work: PhysEDA (arXiv preprint, 2026).

PhysEDA: Physics-Aware Learning Framework for Efficient EDA with Manhattan Distance Decay
Zetao Yang
arXiv preprint · 2026 · Sole author
arXiv Code

A unified physics-aware framework injecting Manhattan-distance decay into both the architecture (PSLA — linear attention) and the training stage (PBRS — reward shaping). Evaluated across decoupling-capacitor placement, macro placement, and IR-drop prediction. On 100×100 grids, the PSLA encoder ran 14× faster and used 98.5% less memory than softmax attention in the reported experiments.
